How were the pyramids built? Bricks, ramps and workers

What we know about how the Egyptians built their pyramids: mud brick and stone, ramps, sledges, the Nile and the paid workers who did it, and how Pyramid Craft turns it into a game.

Mud brick first, then stone

Mastabas

The first royal tombs in Egypt were mastabas: flat, rectangular buildings of sun-dried mud brick over a burial shaft. Mud from the Nile, mixed with straw or sand, was pressed into wooden moulds and dried in the sun, much as it still is in villages along the river.

The step pyramid

Around 2670 BC the architect Imhotep stacked ever smaller mastaba-like layers for King Djoser at Saqqara, in stone. The result, the Step Pyramid, is the oldest large stone building in Egypt, and it stood inside a long panelled enclosure wall.

The true pyramids

About a century later the pyramids at Dahshur and Giza got smooth sides. The Great Pyramid of Khufu was built of some 2.3 million limestone blocks and cased in fine white Tura limestone that once made it shine. Later, in the Middle Kingdom, many pyramids went back to a mud-brick core under a stone casing.

Ramps, sledges and the Nile

Ramps

Nobody left a manual, so how blocks went up is still argued: straight ramps, ramps zigzagging up one face, or ramps wrapping around the pyramid. In 2018 archaeologists found a steep ancient ramp with post holes and stairs at the Hatnub alabaster quarry, a hint of how heavy loads were hauled up slopes.

Sledges and wet sand

Blocks were dragged on wooden sledges. A wall painting in the tomb of Djehutihotep shows a man pouring water in front of a sledge, and lab tests have shown that wet sand roughly halves the pull needed.

The river

Stone from far quarries came by boat, and papyri found at Wadi al-Jarf (the diary of an official called Merer) describe shipping Tura limestone to Giza along canals from the Nile.

Who built them

Paid crews, not slaves

Excavations of the workers' town south of the Giza plateau found bakeries, breweries, barracks and the workers' own tombs, some with notes of their crew names. The builders were organised, fed and paid Egyptians, many of them farmers working during the Nile flood, when the fields were under water.

How Pyramid Craft turns it into a game

Everything is brick

To keep one simple loop, every monument in the game is laid in bricks you make yourself, even the stone ones. The Mudbrick Tomb is mud brick, the Sky Stairway pale limestone, the Great Pyramid white Tura limestone.

The order of history

You build in the same order Egypt did: a mastaba-like tomb, then a stepped pyramid, then a smooth-sided great pyramid with a white casing and a gilded capstone.

Ramps, carts and crews

Ramps, a hand cart, water wheels and workers with jobs stand in for the real ramps, sledges, boats and crews. It is a game, not a reconstruction, but every part has its root in the real story.
