Choosing a Prayer Times App: 7 Things to Check

What makes a prayer times app trustworthy: calculation method, Asr setting, notifications, privacy, ads, Qibla and offline use. A practical checklist.

Prayer times apps can disagree by several minutes, and some are full of ads or ask for more data than they need. These seven points help you pick one you can rely on every day.

1. The calculation method

Fajr and Isha depend on the sun's angle below the horizon, and organizations use different angles (Muslim World League, ISNA, Umm al-Qura, Egyptian, Diyanet and others). A good app chooses a sensible method for your country and lets you change it to match your mosque.

2. The Asr setting

Asr starts when a shadow reaches its object's length (the majority view) or twice its length (Hanafi). Check that the app lets you pick.

3. Notifications that arrive

Look for adhan or reminder notifications that work even if you don't open the app every day, and an easy way to turn each prayer on or off.

4. Privacy

Times only need your approximate location. Prefer apps that calculate on your phone and don't send your location or build a profile of you.

5. Ads

A prayer app is opened at quiet moments. Full-screen ads before the Qur'an or at adhan time are a reason to look elsewhere.

6. A Qibla compass you can calibrate

Phone compasses drift near metal and magnetic cases. A good app explains how to calibrate (a figure-8 movement).

7. Works offline

Travel and weak signal happen. Times calculated on the device keep working without a connection.

Questions

Why do prayer time apps show different times?

Mostly because of the calculation method (the sun angle for Fajr and Isha) and the Asr setting. Choose the same method your mosque uses.
