Reaction Time Test: How Fast Are Your Reflexes?

Test your reaction time right here: tap when the box turns green. What is a normal reaction time, why it changes and how to get faster.

What is a normal reaction time?

For a simple test like this one, where you react to one signal you are already waiting for, most adults land somewhere between 200 and 300 milliseconds. Around 250 ms is typical; under 200 ms is very quick.

A screen adds its own delay: the display, the touch sensor and the browser all take a few milliseconds, so the same person can score differently on different devices. Compare yourself on the same phone, not with a friend's laptop.

What changes your reaction time

  • Sleep and tiredness: a short night slows you down more than almost anything else.
  • Warming up: the first few tries are usually the slowest.
  • Attention: looking straight at the spot where the signal comes is faster than looking around it.
  • Choices: when you must decide what to do (tap the green, not the yellow), reaction time grows.

How to get faster

  1. Rest your finger just above the screen instead of lifting it from far away.
  2. Don't guess the moment: anticipating leads to false starts, which cost more than a slow tap.
  3. Practise little and often; short daily sessions beat one long one.
  4. Play against friends: a little rivalry makes almost everyone faster.

Quick Wits turns this into a party game: in Quick Draw two or more players wait for green and the first to tap wins, with a yellow bait to catch the jumpy ones. Balloon Pop, Star or Bomb and Colour Trap test reflexes with a decision on top. Timing is measured on each player's own phone, so a slow connection never costs points.
